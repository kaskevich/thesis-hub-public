Three layers that carry habitat-inventory attributes per polygon are not yet included: GI polygons with Annex I codes, MSPA elements as polygons, and abandoned semi-natural grassland. They will be added when the redistribution terms of their source are confirmed. Their aggregates are already in the tables, and the indicators derived from them are in the hexagon layers.

What the data are

Green Infrastructure (GI)
Forest and Natura 2000 habitat polygons, rasterised at 20 m: the input of the MSPA. The permanent-grassland polygons of the GI layer are not in the MSPA input.
MSPA
GuidosToolbox 2.5, 8-neighbour connectivity, EdgeWidth 12 cells (240 m), run per county and clipped to the 3 km zone.
Elements
Cores = MSPA classes 3, 17, 35, 67 (core with its edge); corridors = bridges (33); branches = 1, 101; islets = 9, 109.
Hexagon indicators
Within each 500 m hexagon, the share of the GI area affected by tree cover loss, grassland abandonment, imperviousness increase and fragmentation (branch and islet cells), plus road length per GI area. Each indicator is min–max normalised over the 19,741 GI hexagons. Their sum, normalised again, is the cumulative impact.
Hot spots
Getis–Ord local G of the cumulative impact: queen contiguity, binary weights, focal hexagon excluded; classes from two-sided p-values at 0.01, 0.05 and 0.10.
Coordinates
L-EST97 (EPSG:3301). The Copernicus-derived rasters keep their ETRS89-LAEA grid (EPSG:3035).

Every parameter, with the evidence for it in the files: methods and parameters. Every field: data dictionary.

Comparison with the article

165 numbers from Tables 3–5, Figs 5, 6, 7 and 9 and the text were compared with the values recomputed from the released files. 107 match within one unit of the last published digit, 12 are within 5 %, 38 differ and 8 could not be reproduced.

  • Reproduced: all landscape metrics of Table 4; forest loss and abandonment in Table 5; the county element areas in the text; the Dunn z values of Fig. 9; the global G and its standard deviate; and the local G and class of every hot-spot hexagon.
  • MSPA edge width is 12 cells = 240 m, not 200 m.
  • Hot-spot statistic is Gi (focal hexagon excluded), not Gi*.
  • Table 4: the "3 km coastline total area" column holds whole-county areas, and the metrics describe core patches.
  • Element areas in the text differ from Fig. 5 for Natura 2000 cores (58,447 vs 68,147 ha), forest corridors (58,920 vs 68,521 ha) and Natura 2000 corridors (31,767 vs 39,208 ha).
  • Table 5: the Tallinn row repeats the Lääne-Viru values for fragmentation and impervious surfaces, and the road density values could not be reproduced.
  • "Z = −19.94" attributed to GI fragmentation (Ida-Viru vs Tallinn) is the road density test for Saare vs Tallinn area.
  • Table 3 repeats a hand-typed table with no calculation trail.

Reproduce

Python 3.10+ with numpy, pandas, scipy, geopandas, pyogrio, shapely 2 and rasterio. Unzip the parts into one folder, then:

cd code/reproduce
python3 r1_gi_and_mspa.py          # GI and MSPA areas, core-patch metrics (Table 4)
python3 r2_hexagon_indicators.py   # per-hexagon counts from the rasters and roads
python3 r3_statistics.py           # normalisation, Kruskal-Wallis and Dunn, hot spots, county totals
python3 r4_reconcile.py            # comparison with the published values

Each script runs in under a minute with about 2 GB of memory. The original R scripts of the article are in code/original.
